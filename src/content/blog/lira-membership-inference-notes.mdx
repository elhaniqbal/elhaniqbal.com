Membership inference attacks ask whether a specific example was part of a model's training data. A model can have a decent AUC while still being much more concerning in a narrow operating region.

That is why low false-positive-rate metrics matter.

For privacy attacks, a high true-positive rate at a very low false-positive rate can be a stronger signal than broad average performance. It means the attacker can make confident claims about a smaller set of examples.

## Engineering lesson

Evaluation code needs to make score orientation, thresholds, and plots explicit. I learned to check whether higher scores mean “more likely member” or “less likely member,” and to flip scores when needed before comparing ROC curves.

Small evaluation mistakes can make research results look better, worse, or completely inverted.
